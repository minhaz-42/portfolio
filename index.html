I work on making multimodal systems cheaper and better calibrated: deciding when a large vision-language model is actually needed, and when a model should decline to answer instead of guessing.

That work runs along four threads: efficient inference over video with vision-language models, calibrated abstention in closed-book language models, empirical audits of quantum machine learning, and low-cost sensing on microcontrollers. It has produced one accepted paper, one under review, and several manuscripts in preparation. Alongside the research I build and ship software, from local-first web applications and browser tools to a 3D game, with source code and live sites linked under Projects.

About

I am finishing a B.Sc. in Computer Science and Engineering at North South University, in the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering. Most of my work over the past year has been research: two course-based research projects, one accepted shared-task system paper, one conference paper under review, and several independent investigations that I run end to end, from literature audit through experiments to manuscript.

The questions I keep returning to are about cost and honesty in machine learning systems. Which inputs actually need an expensive model? When is a confident answer unjustified? What does a reported gain rest on once the obvious confounds are controlled for? That last question is why several of my projects are audits rather than proposals, and why some of them report negative results. In my directed research, the quantum classification head reaches accuracy parity with its matched classical control and cuts calibration error, but the gain cannot be attributed to the circuit, because the quantum head also carries a learnable output temperature the control lacks. The report says so plainly.

I am aiming at graduate study in computer vision and multimodal learning. My current direction is efficient inference over video, and uncertainty that tracks whether a belief is still true rather than only how confident the model was when it formed it.

Research projects

Frame-Aware Multi-Model Scheduling for Efficient Video Captioning

Complete

CSE/EEE 499 Senior Design Project, Department of ECE, North South University. Lead author of four.

Routes video captioning between a 4B and a 27B vision-language model with a small complexity scorer trained without human labels, so the large model is spent only on the windows that need it. It answers the benchmark in fewer requests than either model alone while scoring above the small one on every judged axis.

Quantum-Enhanced Driver Distraction Detection

Complete

CSE 498R Directed Research, Department of ECE, North South University

A hybrid classical-quantum pipeline with an entropy gate and a continuous risk score, built to test whether a variational quantum head buys anything over a matched classical one. Under a protocol where only the head differs, the quantum head reaches accuracy parity and cuts expected calibration error by 37% relative. The report also states what the evidence does not support: no accuracy gain, no added robustness under noise, and a calibration gain that cannot be attributed to the circuit.

Observing Is Not Free: the decision value of visual observations in vision-language agents

In preparation

When a vision-language agent chooses what to look at, does its selection criterion rank observations the way their realised effect on the answer does? Measured with a counterfactual table of the model's answer before and after every candidate observation, 8.2% help, 5.9% actively harm, and decision value turns out not to be predictable from crop geometry at all.

Knowing What You No Longer Know: calibrated belief validity for persistent world memory

In preparation

Systems that report a confidence say how sure they are what an object is, not how sure they are that a belief is still true. This separates the two, and builds a benchmark from elapsed-time-matched pairs over real and simulated changing rooms to score it. A hazard fitted to real observation gaps predicts validity where recency is close to chance.

SimCert: a classical-simulability audit of trained QNLP text models

Abstract submitted

Non-archival abstract submitted to QTML 2026

When a quantum natural language processing model reports a gain on text, was the quantum part load-bearing, or did the trained circuit sit in the efficiently classically-simulable regime the whole time? Trained circuits are re-simulated under matrix-product-state truncation and an entanglement-removal ablation to find out.

Sequential Video Belief

In progress

Does a video-language model's own previously stated answer impair its ability to revise that answer when later visual evidence contradicts it? An empirical investigation with the go or no-go rule fixed before analysis.

Uncertainty-Guided Language-Grounded 3D Scene Graphs from Sparse Views

In progress

Frozen geometry and vision-language foundation models with a lightweight graph reasoning module, for object-level 3D segmentation and spatial relations from only a few casual images.

Publications

Accepted 2026

Closed-Book Knowledge Base Construction with Calibrated Abstention and Numeric Self-Consistency

Tanvir Ahmed
LM-KBC 2026 shared task (5th edition), Automated Knowledge Base Construction (AKBC) at EMNLP 2026

A single-author system paper on set-valued knowledge base construction under a strict closed-book budget: an open-weight model of at most 32B parameters, no retrieval and no fine-tuning. The system treats deciding when to answer as a first-class decision and beats the official baseline on five of the six relations.
